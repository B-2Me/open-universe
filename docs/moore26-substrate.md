# The moore26 Substrate: Mechanics of a Conserved Discrete Vacuum

**Abstract** — The `moore26` engine is a discrete cellular automaton
that models the quantum vacuum not as an empty coordinate space,
but as a dense, strictly conserved thermodynamic medium. Operating
on a 26-neighbor Moore grid restricted to an even-parity
Face-Centered Cubic (FCC) transport sub-lattice, the engine
replaces continuous field equations with local, integer-based
causal transactions.

Through closed-box ledger auditing, the substrate conserves energy
to 99.999% over 500 ticks: all fractional routing truncations are
explicitly booked as thermodynamic entropy (`heat`) and the sole
exit channel is a designed per-node radiation vent (`h>>7`, 127
units — the physical entropy sink). The remaining ±5-unit residual
is register-precision dust: the substrate's intrinsic quantization
noise floor.

Within this environment, macroscopic constants are not hardcoded
but emerge from structural rate equilibria. The speed of light
(`v/c = 1.00` hop/tick) emerges from unit transport lag. Quantum
bound states — such as the 519-winding dimer — form via a "surplus
toll" mechanism, where transient kinetic flux pays a friction cost
to travel along established topological rails without eroding the
core bond. The engine natively finds a scale-invariant specific
density for matter: an intensive mass-to-bond accretion
equilibrium of 47:161 (~0.285) across a 4× size range.

The `moore26` architecture demonstrates that inertia, binding
energy, linear superposition, and the First Law of Thermodynamics
can arise from the asymmetric deposition and chained volumetric
routing of discrete kinetic flux.
