## The Death of the Continuous Derivative

In legacy physics, velocity is a smooth, continuous slide across a void, defined by the derivative $v = \frac{dx}{dt}$[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span). Because it assumes $\Delta t$ can become infinitely small, it assumes motion is frictionless[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span).

In the active field, motion is not a slide; it is a localized sequence of discrete actualizations[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span). A state change must be handed off from one adjacent node to the next[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span). The causal math replaces the continuous derivative with the discrete step function of **Propagation Friction**[span_12](start_span)[span_12](end_span). Every time a relational state advances by one Planck length ($\Delta x$), it incurs a baseline thermodynamic cost ($\mu$)[span_13](start_span)[span_13](end_span). 

The continuous path integral is replaced by a discrete summation of network routing hops[span_14](start_span)[span_14](end_span):
$$E_{dissipated} = \sum_{n=1}^{N} \mu \cdot \Delta x_n$$

You cannot divide space smaller than the fundamental grain of reality, and the sequence of actualization is strictly bound by the minimum temporal duration (Planck time)[span_15](start_span)[span_15](end_span).

$$\mathbf{S}_{t+1} = \mathbf{S}_t - \nabla_{disc} \Phi(\mathbf{S}_t) - \mu \mathbf{I}$$
