# Topology: Shell-World vs. Knot-World

The defining premise of the Open Universe framework is that **geometry is the dashboard, not the territory.** The physical universe does not care about shapes; it cares about the thermodynamic accounting of relational friction, phase locks, and routing impedance.

To prove this, the Planck Field engine compiles two distinct universes: an 8-fold (Octagonal) substrate and a 6-fold (Hexagonal) substrate. **The underlying physical laws, thermal limits, and code logic are identical in both** — one C source, one `-DTOPOLOGY_HEX` compile flag. The only variable is the coordination number: how many neighbors a node routes traffic to.

By running identical thermodynamic accounting on two different graphs, we observe a radical emergence: changing the adjacency graph shifts the physical universe from classical fluid mechanics to quantum string dynamics.

## 1. The Substrate Axiom: Invariant Viscosity

To ensure an honest comparison, the engine enforces a strict topological invariant for heat diffusion. The "viscosity" of a relational channel is an absolute constant: a channel always transfers exactly $1/9$ of a node's heat per tick.

$$ Heat_{kept} = Heat_{total} - \left(N_{neighbors} \times \frac{Heat_{total}}{9}\right) $$

This reveals an immediate, emergent property of physical space: **sparse-channel matter runs hotter.** Because a 6-fold node only has 6 avenues to dump heat, it natively retains $3/9$ of its heat locally, compared to an 8-fold node's $1/9$. In our simulator, this means 6-fold matter sustains slightly higher internal thermodynamic pressure, eroding ~17% faster under identical ambient quantum foam.

## 2. Oct-8: Macroscopic Continua ("Shell-World")

On the 8-fold substrate, the native stable structure is an octagonal closed streamline.

- **Volumetric Tori:** Matter bounds itself as a continuous, 2D shell (a dissipative torus).
- **Viscous Erosion:** When subjected to ambient quantum foam or radiation, the 8-fold shell degrades *continuously*. The band uniformly thins and stretches, exactly mirroring the classical fluid mechanics of a smoke ring or a dissipating vortex in water.

## 3. Hex-6: Quantum String Dynamics ("Knot-World")

On the 6-fold substrate, the volumetric torus cannot exist. The native stable structure collapses into a 1-dimensional braided graph loop. This universe perfectly mirrors the discrete, non-linear mechanics of subatomic quantum fields.

- **Spiral Accretion:** Like real-world crystalline growth, matter cannot accrete from head-on convergence (which friction-heats and evaporates). It strictly requires angular momentum—spiral deposition—to build persistent structures.
- **Quantized Severing (String Breaking):** When the 1D filament is struck by a massive ~70k-quanta shockwave, it does not thin out like a fluid. It absorbs the impact completely intact — 300 of 300 loop cells hold through the collision. However, as the thermal debris field chews through it, the filament suffers *quantized severing*—individual links are cleanly cut. This is a direct computational analogue to **Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD)**, where pulling quarks apart causes the color-flux string to snap rather than stretch infinitely.
- **The Wounded Arc:** Because Phase Lock and waveguide properties hold the remaining graph together, the severed hex-loop doesn't cascade into total dissolution. It stabilizes into a permanent, non-trivial topological remnant—a physical scar proving the resilience of the Phase Lock mechanism.

## 4. The Empirical Bridge: Graphene Waveguides

The hex-native engine is not just a mathematical curiosity; it maps directly to empirical observations in 2D quantum materials.

In real-world physics, materials like graphene possess a rigid 6-fold hexagonal carbon lattice. Recent laboratory measurements of self-assembled Graphene Nanowrinkles (GNWrs) demonstrate that forcing a hexagonal lattice into sub-nanometer asymmetric curvature creates a massive 1D topological conduit.

Researchers probing these 6-fold wrinkles found a hard threshold voltage ($\sim 1\text{V}$) that acts as a barrier, causing highly asymmetric current flow depending on the bias direction. In the Planck Field, this is the exact measurement of **Phase Friction**. Pushing current "against" the 6-fold curvature-induced phase lock causes a massive spike in metabolic dissipation, creating the threshold barrier. The 6-fold engine's ability to natively precipitate stable 1-D filaments and persistent arcs perfectly matches how real hexagonal nanostructures behave under stress.
